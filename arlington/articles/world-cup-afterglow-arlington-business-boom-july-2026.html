World Cup Afterglow: How Arlington's Local Businesses Are Riding the Wave

From record hotel revenues to packed patios, the tournament's economic impact is still rippling through The American Dream City

July 15, 2026 Arlington Pulse Staff

Photo by Ryan Jacobson on Unsplash

The final whistle has blown on Arlington's historic FIFA World Cup run, but the economic momentum is just getting started. With nine matches drawing hundreds of thousands of visitors to AT&T Stadium and the Entertainment District, local businesses are reporting a summer season unlike any other.

Record-Breaking Numbers

Mayor Jim Ross recently revealed that the World Cup generated roughly half as much revenue for Arlington in just one month as AT&T Stadium typically produces in an entire year. Hotel occupancy hit record highs throughout June, with visitors from across the globe filling rooms from the Entertainment District to Downtown.

But the real story is what happened after the matches ended. Local restaurants, coffee shops, and bars saw traffic surges that extended well beyond game days, as soccer fans explored everything Arlington has to offer.

Where Locals and Visitors Ate

Throughout the tournament, several Arlington establishments became go-to spots for both visiting fans and locals looking to join the excitement:

The Tipsy Oak

This Downtown Arlington gastropub became a pre-game headquarters for fans heading to AT&T Stadium. Their patio was packed with supporters wearing jerseys from Mexico, Brazil, Spain, and France, all bonding over craft cocktails and elevated pub fare.

→ Visit thetipsyoak.com

Hurtado Barbecue

Lines stretched down the block as barbecue enthusiasts from around the world discovered why Texas brisket has legendary status. Their downtown location became a pilgrimage site for food-loving fans.

→ Visit hurtadobbq.com

Division Brewing

This local brewery transformed into an international meeting ground, where supporters from opposing teams found common ground over locally crafted pints. The brewery's communal tables hosted conversations in multiple languages.

→ Visit divisionbrewing.com

4 Kahunas Tiki Lounge

Offering a tropical escape from the Texas heat, this tiki lounge's signature cocktails provided the perfect cooldown after hours in the stadium sun.

→ Visit 4kahunas.com

Nehemiah Coffee Co

The morning headquarters for fans recovering from late matches, with expertly crafted lattes and breakfast offerings fueling another day of World Cup excitement.

→ Visit nehemiahcoffee.com

The Downtown Difference

While the Entertainment District grabbed headlines with its massive crowds, Downtown Arlington experienced its own renaissance. The Urban Union District's walkable streets and local businesses proved that Arlington's appeal extends far beyond the stadium gates.

Local coffee shops like Arlington Coffee Roasters and Inclusion Coffee reported meeting fans from countries they'd never served before, turning casual coffee runs into cultural exchanges.

Looking Ahead

With Arlington leaders already discussing bids for future international events—including the 2031 Women's World Cup—the infrastructure and global connections built during this tournament position the city for continued growth.

The World Cup proved that Arlington can host the world. Now it's up to local businesses to keep that momentum going, welcoming both returning visitors and new residents discovering why The American Dream City is becoming one of Texas's most dynamic destinations.
